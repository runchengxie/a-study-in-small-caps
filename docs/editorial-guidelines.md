# Reader-facing prose

Apply these guidelines to Chinese and English research pages, summaries, chart captions, source notes and documentation. Preserve source identities, measurements, evidence stages and material uncertainty when editing.

Lead with the current judgment or the reader's next useful action, then give the supporting evidence. State the subject and main verb early. Use ordinary explanatory sentences and keep the sample, dates and limitations close to the claim they qualify.

For Chinese prose, state the finding directly. Do not invent a mistaken view to overturn with constructions such as 不是A而是B, 不在于A而在于B, 你以为, 表面上其实, 与其不如 or A不重要. Describe actual research limits plainly, including when returns or capacity still require validation. Never remove a limitation merely to avoid a negative sentence.

Avoid three or more parallel rhetorical clauses, personified abstractions, nominalized actions and promotional language. Use full-width Chinese punctuation in Chinese sentences. Avoid em dashes, emphasis quotes and semicolons. Reserve prose colons for introducing a person's exact words. Preserve URLs, code, identifiers and source titles where exact identity matters.

Review sentences in context after searching for suspect wording. Automated matches are prompts for review, not permission to replace punctuation in machine fields or change the meaning of a source. Keep bilingual claims aligned and leave frozen source assets unchanged.
